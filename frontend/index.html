The records

Every record
keeps its history

An airline is never allowed to forget. When a flight is cancelled, a seat is reassigned or a captain is promoted, the old record is not deleted. It is given a new status and kept, so months later someone can still ask what was true at 05:30. Pick a type of record below and click any row to see its fields and everything that ever happened to it.

Decisions the
airline makes

Swapping an aircraft, replacing a captain, cancelling a flight: each is an operation with a rule behind it. A pilot without the right licence cannot be assigned, a grounded aircraft cannot fly. Try one below: fill in the record ids and press Run. If a rule says no, you will see why in red, and that refusal is kept in the history too.

Eight stories from the day

When plans
go wrong

A hydraulic leak found at 04:45, a captain who calls in sick, fog at an airport, a passport that is not valid. Each story starts with a normal day, breaks it, and shows how the airline recovers. It then checks that the old records are still there. Press Run full day at the top to play all eight in order.

Scroll

Each story is written to run in order on the same day. Grey lines are things that happened; refused lines are rules deliberately stopping a bad action; check lines are questions asked afterwards to prove the history survived.

Close of business

The end of
day report

The question management asks at the end of the day: what changed today, who was affected, and why? This page answers it from the records alone. It tells which aircraft each flight ended up on, which disruptions happened and what options were considered, what was refused, and what is still unresolved for the next day.

Read the flight table first: the aircraft chain column shows every aircraft a flight was planned on, and the passenger column shows how many ended up flown, rebooked, offloaded or cancelled.

49 classes · four inheritance families

How the
model is built

Everything on this site is a Python class. Some classes are specialised versions of others — a flight-deck pilot is a kind of crew member, who is a kind of employee, who is a kind of person — and that family tree is drawn below, read live from the code. Next to it is the full list of operations, and beneath that, every class with what it represents.

Scroll

Deeper colour in the tree means a deeper level of inheritance. The one four-level chain — Record → Person → Employee → CrewMember → FlightDeckCrew — is the multi-level structure the brief asks for.
