# Assumptions (submission item 2)

Where the real-world domain or the specification is ambiguous, this is what the model assumes.
Each assumption is visible in the code at the place named.

## Answers to "Questions the Specification Does Not Answer"

| # | Question | Decision | Where in code |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planned service vs flight on a date vs aircraft movement | Three different records. `ScheduledService` is the timetable entry (PK301 LHE-KHI 05:30 daily). `Flight` is PK301 on 24 Sep 2026 - bookings, crew, bags, cargo and disruptions attach here. `FlightMovement` is what physically happened (actual times, actual destination) and is created only at departure. A diversion changes the movement, never the plan. | `network.py` |
| 2 | Is a charter the same kind of record as a scheduled service? | Yes. A charter is a *commercial arrangement*, so `ScheduledService.service_kind = "charter"` and the customer contract is a separate `CharterAgreement`. The flight still needs an aircraft, crew, dispatch and can be delayed or diverted exactly like any other. A `CharterFlight` subclass was considered and rejected (see rejected.md #2). | `network.py`, `commercial.py` |
| 3 | What belongs to the airline, the airport, or a third-party handler? | `Airport` and `AirportRestriction` are owned by the airport authority (`Airport.operator`); the airline only reads them. Bags and cargo record *who* accepted/handled them (`accepted_by`, `handler`) which can be an `Organisation` of kind `handler` rather than an employee. Everything else is airline data. | `network.py`, `baggage.py`, `people.Organisation` |
| 4 | When an aircraft configuration changes, do old bookings point to the new or the old configuration? | The old one. `SeatAssignment` stores the `Seat`, which lives inside a specific `CabinConfiguration` version. Re-configuration ends the old configuration (`status = superseded`), creates a new one, and *re-seats* affected passengers by superseding their assignments - the old assignment is never edited. | `fleet.CabinConfiguration`, `operations.reconfigure_cabin`, `reseat_flight` |
| 5 | Can one employee hold several capacities; how are historical role changes kept? | Job title is a dated `RoleAssignment`, not a subclass and not a field. Promotion ends the old role and opens a new one. `CrewAssignment.role_title` is frozen at assignment time so the record shows the title held *on the day*. Qualifications are separate dated `Qualification` records because a title does not imply a qualification (the scenario's "correct job title but no type rating"). | `people.py`, `operations.change_role` |
| 6 | Which similarities are genuine inheritance? | Four hierarchies survived analysis: Person→Employee→CrewMember→FlightDeck/Cabin (identity → employment → licensing → what "qualified" means); `TemporalRecord` (records true for a period); `EngineeringRecord` → Inspection/Defect/WorkOrder (tech-log entries); `Disruption` → Delay/Cancellation/Diversion/Substitution (ops-control events with a decision and affected parties). Baggage/cargo, charter/scheduled, deferred/normal defect and title-based employee subclasses were rejected. | inheritance.md, rejected.md |
| 7 | Should cancelled records be deleted, marked inactive, or transformed? | Marked with a status and kept, never deleted. Cancelling a flight sets every dependent record to a `cancelled*` status and stores counts of what was preserved on the `Cancellation` record. Rebooking *transforms* by creating a new segment linked both ways (`rebooked_from` / `rebooked_to`). | `operations.cancel_flight`, `rebook_segment` |
| 8 | How much of the real aviation rule set is modelled? | Only the rules the scenario needs: type rating / cabin qualification per aircraft type, a single 13-hour daily duty limit, rest/leave periods, positioning, MEL deferral only by a certifying engineer on that type, grounding defects cannot be deferred, six-month passport validity for international travel, bags come off when their owner is offloaded. Everything else is recorded as an assumption below rather than coded. | `operations.py` |

## Other assumptions

1. **One operating day.** The seed is Thursday 24 Sep 2026; the simulated clock starts at 04:00 and the demos move it forward. Dates on records are real `date`/`datetime` values so the model is not limited to one day.
2. **A seat exists only inside one configuration version.** Reconfiguration creates new `Seat` records; the old ones remain so old seat assignments still point at a real seat.
3. **Infants** are segments with cabin class `INF` and no seat; they carry an `INFT` special-service request and are attached to the adult's booking.
4. **Cargo capacity** is a single weight figure per airframe (`Aircraft.cargo_capacity_kg`). Volume, ULD positions and weight-and-balance are out of scope.
5. **Aircraft position** (`Aircraft.location`) is updated on arrival. **Crew position** (`CrewMember.position_location`) is the base plus explicit positioning moves; the model treats a crew member as available at an airport if they are based there or arrive there on an earlier sector of the same day. It does not infer where crew physically are from flown sectors (see limitations.md).
6. **Delays are declared, not computed.** `declare_delay` moves ETD/ETA and then identifies knock-on flights that share the aircraft or crew within 45 minutes, and connecting passengers with under 60 minutes. Real minimum connection times vary by airport.
7. **Recovery options are records.** Ops control writes down each candidate choice as a `RecoveryOption`; choosing one marks the others `rejected` but keeps them so the end-of-day report can show what was considered.
8. **Dangerous goods declared after acceptance** put the consignment on `held`, and offload it from any passenger flight it was planned on, pending re-check. The original acceptance record is untouched; the `HandlingRequirement` is dated so the model shows *when* the airline learned of it.
9. **Handling requirements are dated** (`TemporalRecord`) so a temperature requirement can also be relaxed or corrected later without losing the original.
10. **Airport restrictions carry a capacity factor** but the model does not automatically cancel flights; ops control decides and the decision is recorded as a disruption.
11. **Retiring an aircraft** ends its current assignments on planned flights and puts those flights in `needs_aircraft`. It does not auto-substitute; that is an ops decision.
12. **Business rules refuse, they do not silently fix.** A refused attempt raises `DomainError` and is logged on the record involved, so the audit trail includes what was *tried*.
13. **Money** is limited to fares, charter price and a recovery option cost estimate; no ledger, refunds or invoicing.
14. **Identity** is one `Person` per real person. A person is either a `Passenger` or an `Employee` in this model; an employee travelling as a passenger would need a `Passenger` record (documented limitation).
